Akarachi Etinosa Amadi is a chemical engineer who built companies in upstream oil and gas, and gives through the Akarachi Foundation. At 28 he was also elected to speak for Mbaitoli and Ikeduru.

Read his story

Engineer

Chemical engineering at Covenant University, then training with the Nigerian Petroleum Development Company. He worked on drilling, dredging and land reclamation across the Niger Delta.

Businessman

He founded and runs several companies across different sectors, most of them in upstream oil and gas.

Philanthropist

Through the Akarachi Foundation: homes for families without one, exam fees and scholarships, hospital bills settled, tools for traders.

Engineer first. Then businessman. Then lawmaker.

  1. Eziama

    A son of Ikeduru

    He comes from Eziama in Ikeduru Local Government Area. School took him to Warri and then to Remo in Ogun State.

  2. Covenant

    Trained as a chemical engineer

    A Bachelor of Engineering in Chemical Engineering from Covenant University, Ota, specialising in kinetic molecular engineering. He trained with the Nigerian Petroleum Development Company and worked in oil and gas operations across the Niger Delta.

  3. Enterprise

    Built companies before he ran

    He founded and runs several companies, most of them in upstream oil and gas, and served as Vice President of the Network for African Student Entrepreneurs.

  4. 2022

    Paid for the first JAMB forms

    The Akarachi Foundation began covering exam fees for students at home. The scheme has run every year since and is now in its fifth edition.

  5. 2023

    Elected at 28

    He won the Mbaitoli/Ikeduru seat and entered the 10th National Assembly as one of its youngest members. By October his first five bills were on the order paper. He chairs the House Committee on Oceans and Fisheries.

  6. 2026

    From vision to result

    Phase one of Operation Light Up Mbaike put up 2,500 solar streetlights. Champion Newspapers named him Legislator of the Year. In April, 67 transformers went out to 63 communities in a single day.

What the giving looks like at home

Some he paid for himself, through the Akarachi Foundation. Some is public work he brought home. All of it can be pointed to on a map.

See programmes you can apply for

  • Free JAMB and post-UTME registration every year since 2022, and full scholarships to public universities for students who score 200 or above. Ninety-two scholars so far, split evenly between Mbaitoli and Ikeduru.

  • Sixty-seven 500KVA transformers for 63 autonomous communities, and 2,500 solar streetlights in the first phase of Operation Light Up Mbaike.

  • He personally paid for the road from Ochicha Ngugo to Eziama, and attracted others, including Nkwo Orji to Afor Obazu and Eke-Atta to Ikembara.

  • A bill-offset partnership with the Federal Medical Centre, Owerri, for patients who cannot pay, and a five-day medical outreach across both local governments.

  • New houses built for families who had none.

  • Afor-Di-Mkpa, a family welfare scheme shared across Mbaike without regard to church or party, and integration programmes for market women.

  • The yearly Mbaike Unity Football Cup, whose players have gone on to national and international clubs, alongside tools and start-up support for young traders and artisans.

In the House

Thirty-eight bills from one seat

Since 2023 he has also been the Member for Mbaitoli/Ikeduru, and he chairs the House Committee on Oceans and Fisheries. Colleges, vocational certificates, a football academy for Mbaitoli, fisheries, maritime safety. Each bill is set out below in plain English, with the stage it has reached.

7 of the 7 bills matched to public records so far. Press reports put the full count at 38.

  • HB.5422023

    Federal College of Entrepreneurship and Skill Acquisition, Mbaitoli/Ikeduru (Establishment) Bill

    Creates a federal college at home that teaches trades and business skills, so school leavers can train for work without leaving the constituency.

    First reading

    Order paper, October 2023

  • HB.5432023

    Nigerian Institute for Vocational Training and Certification (Establishment) Bill

    Sets up a national body to train artisans and issue certificates that employers anywhere in Nigeria will recognise.

    First reading

    Order paper, October 2023

  • HB.7292023

    Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety Agency Act (Amendment) Bill

    Updates the law that governs NIMASA, the agency responsible for shipping safety and maritime labour in Nigerian waters.

    First reading

    Read on 24 October 2023

  • HB.7302023

    National Football Academy, Mbaitoli, Imo State (Establishment) Bill

    Sets up a national academy in Mbaitoli to find and train young footballers, with schooling alongside sport.

    First reading

    Read on 24 October 2023

  • Cap. B102023

    Border Communities Development Agency Act (Amendment) Bill

    Strengthens the agency that funds roads, clinics and schools in communities along Nigeria's borders.

    First reading

    Order paper, October 2023

  • HB.25802025

    Inland Fisheries Act (Amendment) Bill

    Modernises the rules for fishing in rivers and lakes, which rural households rely on for food and income.

    First reading

    Read on 12 November 2025

  • 20262026

    Fisheries education and research bills

    A set of bills to fund teaching and research in fisheries, so that fish farming can grow as a trade.

    In progress

    Advanced on the floor, July 2026
